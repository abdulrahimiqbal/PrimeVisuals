# Two-Universes Breakthrough Protocol

This directory records promotion-gated Two-Universes research cycles.

The protocol is deliberately conservative: a result is not a breakthrough
candidate unless every required gate passes. A failed cycle must name the
representation mutation forced by the failure.

## Required Promotion Gates

1. Precise theorem-shaped statement.
2. Exact finite-field interpretation.
3. Integer-prime holdout evidence.
4. Local/null/random controls.
5. Compression / low parameter count.
6. Scale and field stability.
7. Novelty audit.
8. Reproducible code, logs, plots.
9. Plausible proof path.
10. Expert-ready evidence pack.

## Breakthrough Detector Rule

The loop does not promote by visual surprise, correlation size, or novelty vibes.
It can only emit `PROMOTE` when every gate above passes in the generated
protocol ledger. A result may be kept as a lead when it has a precise matched
statistic, exact finite-field object, integer holdout evidence, and reproducible
artifacts, but any failed control, scale, novelty, proof-path, or expert-pack
gate blocks breakthrough status.

Operationally, the loop knows it has a breakthrough candidate only when a
machine-readable audit has:

1. a named object on both sides (`Z` and `F_q[t]`);
2. a formula for the statistic before holdout data is scored;
3. a single family or mechanism that survives in `Z` and in every required
   finite field, without cherry-picking different explanations per universe;
4. preregistered holdout ranges, null models, and local controls;
5. complete scale ladders, including `N=1M,2M,4M,8M` when integer data is used;
6. a novelty/proof audit that rules out known theorem disguises and plotting
   artifacts;
7. reproducible JSON/MD/plot/script artifacts plus an expert-ready pack.

If any one of those checks is false, the loop must write a rejection or
non-promoted lead, then force a representation change.

## Current Next Move

The aggregate transport route is stopped, the Chebotarev new-domain branch is
calibration-only, and the first proof-first algebraic-family branch is exact
calibration. Single-extension proportions, fixed joint Kummer splitting,
low-conductor family covariance, and complete Weierstrass all-parameter trace
sums/complete second moments are all absorbed by their known theorem baselines.
The fixed CM curve mutation also fails: it avoids complete-family
orthogonality, but constant-curve residue fields are determined by degree and
do not match the rational-prime profile.
The incomplete quadratic-twist CM mutation also fails: nonconstant twists avoid
constant-curve degree rigidity, but the signal still factors through CM traces
and quadratic-character windows and does not pass matched-profile controls.
The first non-CM Legendre-family mutation is exact and non-isotrivial, but it is
only a pilot: the integer side does not include the 1M/2M/4M/8M ladder, the
integer profile does not beat controls, and the q=3,5,7 profile spread is too
large for promotion.
The theorem-first Legendre supersingularity mutation is full-scale and
non-point-counting, but it is also a known-theorem trap: the integer residual is
exactly the `p mod 4` supersingularity criterion on the `j=1728` special orbit,
and the field profiles are fixed by q-local congruence and degree effects.
The generic non-CM obstruction map then screens the remaining algebraic-family
routes before data. Complete Legendre moments have exact baselines but zero
residual; fixed generic traces lack a registered finite-field baseline and
full-scale trace engine; incomplete windows lack a theorem target; generic
supersingular counts need class-number/extension-degree baselines; abstract
monodromy trace functions are not concrete enough to score.
The quadratic Dirichlet-character branch is a genuinely new domain after the
algebraic-family stop, but the first full-ladder prime-race statistic is only
calibration: it validates exact integer and polynomial characters, yet the
observed profile is small and does not beat nearby-character, shuffle, or
bootstrap controls.

  low-conductor covariance matrices themselves.
- Do not add more Kummer/Chebotarev cover counts unless the proposal names a
  theorem target that is not just equidistribution calibration.
- Continuing requires a genuinely different domain/object or a proof-first
  lemma smaller than a famous open conjecture.
- Do not promote complete-family identities whose sums telescope by bijection;
  an algebraic-family continuation needs an incomplete-family, higher-moment,
  monodromy, or spectral residual with a nonzero theorem-normalized signal.
- Do not add more complete all-parameter moments whose theorem-normalized
  residual is identically zero by character orthogonality.
- The next algebraic-family object must break completeness or use a genuine
  monodromy/spectral statistic with a nonzero residual after the finite-field
  theorem baseline.
- Do not promote constant curves over F_q[t] whose residue-field profile is
  determined only by degree; a spectral continuation must use nonconstant
  monodromy over F_q(t) or a rigorously preregistered incomplete-family
  residual.
- Do not promote CM twist-family character-window profiles; the next
  algebraic cycle must use non-CM monodromy, a non-isotrivial family over
  F_q(t), or a theorem-first incomplete-family residual with a named nonzero
  baseline.
- Do not promote pilot-scale non-CM Legendre-family trace profiles; the next
  algebraic cycle needs either a faster trace engine for the full integer

- Do not promote supersingular special-orbit signals, `j=1728`/`j=0`
  automorphism-locus signals, or any statistic exactly explained by local

- The next algebraic cycle must leave special automorphism and CM loci; it must

- The algebraic-family branch is stopped unless a concrete generic non-CM

- Registration must name the exact finite-field theorem mechanism, integer

  novelty audit, proof path, and expert-pack criteria before data.
- Without that, keep the research program stopped rather than manufacturing
  another statistic.

No result should be promoted without passing all gates above.
